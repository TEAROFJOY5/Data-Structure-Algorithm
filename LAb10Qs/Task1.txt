# Task 1: 
A small walk-in clinic operates with exactly one doctor on duty for the entire shift, and patients arrive one at a time throughout the day without any advance notice of how many will come or how busy the clinic will get. Every patient who walks through the door is immediately assessed by the triage nurse and assigned a severity score, where a higher score indicates a more critical condition and a lower score indicates a less urgent case. The doctor is required to always treat the most critical patient first, because allowing a high-severity patient to wait behind a low-severity one could have serious medical consequences. At the same time, the clinic follows a strict fairness policy: if two patients happen to have exactly the same severity score, the patient who arrived earlier must be treated first, so that no patient with equal urgency is ever allowed to jump ahead simply because they were registered later than someone else. Because the total number of arrivals is completely unknown at the start of the shift and the waiting line can grow arbitrarily long, the clinic cannot use a fixed-size array to store the queue and instead stores the waiting patients as a dynamically allocated sorted singly linked list, where the list is always maintained so that the front node holds the patient with the highest severity and equal severities retain their original arrival order. Your job is to implement the triage queue that this clinic relies on, making sure that both the priority ordering and the FIFO tie-breaking among equal severities are preserved at all times, and then to simulate one busy afternoon shift in `main` to demonstrate that the queue behaves correctly under a realistic sequence of enrollments and doctor calls.

Requirements:
You must implement a class named `TriageQueue` that uses a sorted singly linked list built from nodes defined as `struct Node { int patientId; int severity; Node* next; };`, where the list is always kept sorted so that the front node is the patient with the highest severity and, among patients with equal severity, the one who arrived earliest appears first. The `enroll(int patientId, int severity)` method must insert a new patient into the correct sorted position, walking the list until it finds the right spot, and it must place the new patient after all existing patients who have the same severity so that arrival order among equals is preserved. The `callNext()` method must remove the front patient from the list, free that node, and return the removed patient's `patientId`; if the queue is empty it must print an appropriate error message and return `-1` without crashing. The `peekCritical()` method must return the `patientId` of the front patient without removing anyone from the list, and it must also handle the empty case by printing a message and returning `-1`. The `printQueue()` method must print every patient in the queue from front to back in the form `(id, severity)` separated by spaces, followed by a newline, and it must print an appropriate message if the queue is empty. You must also write a proper destructor that walks the entire list and frees every node so that no memory is leaked when the `TriageQueue` object goes out of scope. Finally, in `main`, you must simulate the following sequence of events and print the state of the queue after every two operations: first enroll the patients `enroll(101, 3)`, `enroll(102, 7)`, `enroll(103, 3)`, `enroll(104, 9)`, `enroll(105, 7)`, and `enroll(106, 1)`; then print the queue; then call `callNext()` twice, printing the returned id each time; then print the queue again; and finally call `peekCritical()` and print the result.
